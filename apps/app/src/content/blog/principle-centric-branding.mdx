## Wish

> I wish to crystallize and communicate a core principle that defines my approach to building the right software, guiding me and resonating deeply with my audience.

What unique skills, experiences, or perspectives do you bring to your field? What are your personal brand's core messages or themes?

### Identifying Your Core Principle

To find your principle, consider the following aspects of software development where your interests and beliefs might align:

1. **User-Centric Design**: Prioritizing the needs and experiences of the end-user in software development. Your principle could be advocating for a development process that starts and ends with the user, ensuring technology serves humanity effectively and compassionately.
2. **Sustainable Development**: Emphasizing practices that ensure long-term maintainability and scalability of software. This could involve advocating for clean code, comprehensive documentation, and continuous refactoring to prevent technical debt.
3. **Ethical Technology**: Focusing on the ethical implications of software, including privacy, security, and societal impact. Your principle might be promoting transparency, ethical decision-making, and responsibility in technology.
4. **Inclusive and Accessible Technology**: Ensuring software is accessible to all, including people with disabilities. This principle involves championing accessibility standards and inclusive design practices.
5. **Agile and Adaptive Development**: Believing in flexible, iterative development processes that adapt to changing requirements and feedback. Your principle could be about promoting agile methodologies to foster collaboration, efficiency, and resilience in software projects.
6. **Open Source and Collaboration**: Valuing open-source software and the power of community-driven development. This could involve advocating for open standards, sharing knowledge freely, and collaborating across boundaries.

#### Larry Tesler

In the mid '70s at Xerox PARC, Larry Tesler felt personal computing could change how people thought and lived.

At the time, software interfaces were designed around modes, and Larry believed modes turned people off from computers.

This threatened Larry’s dream for what personal computing could be. So he made it his personal mission to eliminate modes from software.

He formed a principle: no person should be trapped in a mode. This principle informed everything that he did.

Create the environment where **no person should be trapped in a mode.**

#### Alan Kay

Alan Kay invented object-oriented programming. His goal was to “amplify human reach and bring new ways of thinking to a faltering civilization that desperately needed it.”

Alan believed if children became fluent in thinking in the medium of the computer, then they'd become adults with new forms of critical thought.

Alan invented on the principle that programming literacy would enlighten society.

#### Richard Stallman

Richard Stallman started the GNU project. His principle is software must be free: software freedom is a matter of right and wrong.

And he’s taken an uncompromising approach to realizing his vision.

Create the environment where **software is free.**

No, she established a principle: women's suffrage. That's who she was. That’s the identity she chose. That was her vision and she brought it to the world.

Influential people dedicate their lives to ideals with a sense of right and wrong.

They often fight against an authority or mainstream that doesn’t recognize their wrong as being wrong.

Create the environment where **software is free.**

### Defining Your Core Messages or Themes

Once you've identified your core principle, distill it into clear, compelling messages that resonate with your target audience. These messages should reflect your unique perspective and offer a clear value proposition to your audience. Consider the following steps to define your core messages:

- **Articulate the Value**: Explain why your principle matters in the context of software development and its broader impact on society, businesses, or individuals.
- **Share Your Journey**: Use your personal experiences, challenges, and successes to illustrate how you've applied your principle in real-world scenarios. This adds authenticity and relatability to your brand.
- **Educate and Inspire**: Offer actionable insights, best practices, and thought leadership on how others can implement or support your principle in their work. This positions you as a go-to expert and a leader in your field.
- **Engage in Dialogue**: Encourage feedback, discussions, and community engagement around your principle. This shows that you value diverse perspectives and are committed to fostering a community around your core message.
